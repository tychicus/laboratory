A strong lead. More work ahead.

These three markers alone do not prove leukemia: normal B-cell precursors can express them too. A real diagnosis combines the complete marker pattern, blood counts and morphology, usually bone marrow evaluation, genetic testing, and clinical findings.

Medical laboratory scientists uncover and validate the evidence; pathologists and the care team integrate the results.

About this case & sources

Sample preparation and instrument quality checks are assumed complete. All three plots display the same simulated cell events: side scatter (horizontal) versus CD45 (vertical), CD34 versus side scatter, and CD19 versus CD10. An elliptical selection on the first plot identifies events that are then highlighted in the other two plots. Dashed marker boundaries illustrate negative and positive regions; they are not clinical cutoffs. All axes and populations are schematic, not calibrated diagnostic data. The selected low-CD45, low-scatter group is a candidate population, not a diagnosis from its position. Marker results come from additional fluorescence measurements. Counts and proportions are simulated.
