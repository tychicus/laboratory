Normal white cells / 03 / MYELOID CELL

Monocytes

Big cells. Many assignments.

Monocytes patrol the blood, respond to inflammation, and can enter tissues. There, some develop into macrophages or other specialized immune cells.

What to look for

Read the cell.

  1. A folded or indented nucleus

    The nucleus often has a kidney-like outline with looser chromatin than a small resting lymphocyte.

  2. Roomy cytoplasm

    Blue-gray cytoplasm may contain fine granules and clear vacuoles. Monocytes are often among the largest cells in a blood film.

On the job

How they protect you.

01

Remove unwanted material

Phagocytosis clears microbes and cellular debris.

02

Relay information

Monocytes and their descendants release signals and can present pieces of antigens to T cells.

03

Work in tissues

Monocyte-derived macrophages help with defense and repair. Many resident macrophages originate before birth and maintain themselves locally.

The laboratory connection

A monocyte in blood and a macrophage in tissue are related, but they are not interchangeable names. Not every tissue macrophage comes from a newly arriving blood monocyte.
