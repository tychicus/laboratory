02 / Common in the U.S.

The tiny worm with a nighttime routine.

Pinworm (Enterobius vermicularis) is the most common worm infection in the United States.

A household hitchhiker

Female worms leave the intestine at night and deposit eggs around the anus. Itching can transfer eggs to fingers, then bedding, objects, or another person’s mouth. Many people have few symptoms; nighttime itching and disrupted sleep are typical clues. Children and their household contacts are frequently affected.

At the laboratory bench

The useful sample is often a morning adhesive-tape collection from the skin around the anus, before washing or using the bathroom. Microscopists look for the characteristic eggs. A routine stool sample is less useful for this parasite.

Break the cycle

Treatment options include pyrantel pamoate, mebendazole, or albendazole. A second dose two weeks later targets worms that hatched after the first dose. Household members and caregivers are generally treated together. Handwashing, short clean fingernails, bathing, and cleaning bedding help prevent reinfection. Young children and pregnancy need clinician-specific guidance.

Why isn’t one dose always enough?

The medicines kill worms but do not reliably kill their eggs. The second treatment catches the next generation.
