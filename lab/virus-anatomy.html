What’s inside
a virus?

Start with genetic instructions and a protective protein coat. Some viruses add a fatty envelope, attachment proteins, and useful enzymes.

01 / Genome

The instructions

DNA or RNA encodes viral proteins. Depending on the virus, the genome can be single-stranded, double-stranded, or split into segments.

02 / Capsid

The protein coat

Repeating protein units protect the genome. Some make geometric shells; others form helices around the genetic material.

03 / Envelope

An optional outer layer

A lipid membrane acquired from a host cell surrounds some viruses. Viral proteins are embedded in it. Non-enveloped viruses lack this layer.

04 / Attachment proteins

The first contact

Surface proteins bind compatible molecules on a host cell. Receptor binding is one factor that determines which cells a virus can infect.

What isn’t in the package?

A virus has no ribosomes to build proteins and no independent cellular metabolism. Some carry enzymes, but they still need a host cell. The envelope is a covering, not a complete cell.
