Hematology / 02

Two loops.
One continuous journey.

Choose a stage and follow its moving arrows through the illustrated vessels. Then continue to the next stop.

Stage 1 of 4

Body → heart

Oxygen-poor blood

Oxygen-poor blood returns.

Veins bring blood from the body into the right atrium. It then passes into the right ventricle, ready for the trip to the lungs.

Main vessels: venae cavae

Stage 2 of 4

Heart → lungs

Oxygen-poor blood

The right heart sends it to the lungs.

The right ventricle pumps blood through the pulmonary arteries to both lungs. At the air sacs, CO₂ leaves the blood and O₂ enters.

Main vessels: pulmonary arteries

Stage 3 of 4

Lungs → heart

Oxygen-rich blood

Oxygen-rich blood comes back.

Oxygen binds to hemoglobin in the lungs. The pulmonary veins carry the blood into the left atrium, then it passes into the left ventricle.

Main vessels: pulmonary veins

Stage 4 of 4

Heart → body

Oxygen-rich blood

The left heart delivers oxygen.

The left ventricle pumps blood through the aorta toward the head and the rest of the body. In tissue capillaries, oxygen leaves the blood and CO₂ enters. Then the journey starts again.

Main vessel: aorta

Blue is a diagram color. All blood is red; oxygen-poor blood is darker red.

Follow direction, not color.

Arteries carry blood away from the heart. Veins carry it toward the heart. That is why pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood.
