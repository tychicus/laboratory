What does this have to do with bacteria?

Bacteria multiply by binary fission: one cell becomes two, then four, then eight. That is the same mathematical pattern as doubling a dollar. In this money example, doubling happens once a day. Under ideal laboratory conditions, some bacteria can divide roughly every 20 minutes.

In nature, bacteria cannot keep doubling forever. Nutrients, space, waste, and other conditions limit growth. The dollar challenge is a mathematical illustration—not a prediction of an infection.
