Normal white cells / 02 / LYMPHOID CELL

Lymphocytes

One appearance. Several specialists.

B cells, T cells, and natural killer cells belong to the lymphocyte family. They work in different ways, even when they look similar on a routine blood film.

What to look for

Read the cell.

  1. A dense, round nucleus

    Many resting lymphocytes are small, with a dark nucleus occupying much of the cell.

  2. A narrow rim of cytoplasm

    A small lymphocyte often has only a thin blue rim. Larger or activated lymphocytes can look very different.

TEACHING ILLUSTRATION
DISTINCT DEVELOPMENTAL ROUTES

B, T, or NK cell

B cells develop in marrow and complete transitional maturation in peripheral lymphoid tissue. T precursors travel to the thymus. NK cells develop in marrow and other lymphoid sites.

TEACHING ILLUSTRATION
BLOOD, LYMPH & TISSUES

Mature specialist

Mature lymphocytes circulate or reside in tissues. B and T cells can expand after activation and form effector and memory populations.

Simplified developmental overview. B, T, and NK cells are separate branches, not consecutive stages. Drawings do not identify a lineage by appearance.

The laboratory connection

You usually cannot reliably tell B cells from T cells on a routine stained smear. Flow cytometry examines combinations of cell markers to separate these populations.
