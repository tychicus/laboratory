B cells

Antibodies. Memory. A lasting response.

B cells recognize antigens through specialized receptors. After activation, some become antibody-secreting plasma cells; others join the memory pool.

BONE MARROW

Pro-B / pre-B cell

Developing B cells assemble their antibody-receptor genes and pass checkpoints.

TEACHING ILLUSTRATION
MARROW → SPLEEN

Immature / transitional B cell

An immature B cell carries surface IgM. Selection helps remove or control self-reactive cells; transitional maturation continues outside the marrow.

TEACHING ILLUSTRATION
LYMPHOID TISSUES

Mature naive B cell

The cell is mature but has not yet encountered its matching antigen in a productive immune response.

TEACHING ILLUSTRATION
AFTER ANTIGEN ENCOUNTER

Activated B cell

With appropriate signals, often including T-cell help, B cells multiply and differentiate. This is activation of a mature cell.

TEACHING ILLUSTRATION
TWO POSSIBLE OUTCOMES

Plasma or memory cell

Plasma cells release antibodies. Memory B cells help the body respond to later encounters. These are branches, not a single linear sequence.

Simplified teaching pathway. Development and antigen-driven activation are different processes. Photographs are representative examples as captioned, not a time-lapse maturation sequence. A routine smear alone does not establish lymphocyte subtype.

On the job

What this specialist does.

01

Recognize

A B-cell receptor binds a particular antigen.

02

Make antibodies

Plasma cells secrete antibodies that can neutralize targets or mark them for other defenses.

03

Remember

Memory cells help support a faster response to a future encounter.

The laboratory connection

A plasma cell is a differentiated B-lineage cell, not a sixth routine blood differential category. Resting B cells cannot reliably be distinguished from resting T cells by a smear alone.
