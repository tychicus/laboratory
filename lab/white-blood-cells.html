At the microscope

Shape gives us the first clues.

Compare the nucleus, cytoplasm, and granules. A blood-film differential sorts white cells into these five main groups; specialized tests can take the investigation further.

Photographs are shown at different crops, not a common size scale. Stain colors vary between preparations.

A shared starting point

Made in the marrow.
Trained for a task.

White blood cells arise from blood-forming stem cells. Developing cells specialize along different routes. Most precursor stages belong in the marrow; T-cell maturation includes the thymus.
