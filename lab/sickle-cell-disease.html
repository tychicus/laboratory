Tiny change. Big impact.

An inherited change in hemoglobin.
A different shape. A problem with blood flow.

Hemoglobin = the oxygen-carrying protein in red cells.

01 / Look closer

Built to bend… or bent out of shape?

Presenter notes

Normal red cells are flexible discs with thinner centers. Sickled cells become stiff and fragile. The smear contains several shapes; not every red cell looks like a sickle.

Presenter notes

An inherited HBB variant changes one amino-acid building block of hemoglobin. When oxygen levels fall, hemoglobin S can form long, rigid fibers inside the cell, distorting its shape.

Presenter notes

Sickled cells can obstruct capillaries and other small blood vessels. Blocked blood flow can cause intense pain, including in bones and joints, and damage organs over time. Cells obstruct blood vessels, not the joint space itself. Red cells also break down faster than the body can replace them, causing anemia.

04 / It runs in genes

Inherited. Not contagious.

If both parents have trait, each pregnancy has:

  • 25%No disease or trait
  • 50%Sickle cell trait
  • 25%Sickle cell disease

Trait ≠ disease

Presenter notes

Each parent passes on one HBB copy. Trait usually means one HbS copy and one normal copy. These percentages are independent chances for each pregnancy—not a prediction for exactly four children. SCD is a group of disorders: HbS can also pair with another disease-causing hemoglobin variant.

Presenter notes

Hemoglobin electrophoresis separates and measures hemoglobin types. The gel photograph shows several samples, not a single patient’s result. Some variants migrate together, so band position alone does not confirm HbS; complementary testing is needed. Genetic testing can clarify the diagnosis. A smear alone does not tell the whole story. In the United States, sickle cell screening is part of newborn screening.

Treatments and possible future cures!

Small cells. Huge science.

Presenter notes

Hydroxyurea can reduce sickling and pain crises. Blood transfusions help treat or prevent some complications. Stem-cell transplants and gene therapies offer the possibility of a cure for selected patients, but require intensive treatment and carry serious risks.
